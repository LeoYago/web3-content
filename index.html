The problem

Cultural access is locked into silos.

Access is tied to isolated platforms, proprietary accounts and subscriptions that don't interoperate. Audiences juggle separate providers; rights holders struggle to express permissions, share revenue and work across channels.

  • Silos
  • Vendor lock-in
  • High barriers for new European players

The solution

Shared infrastructure, not another platform.

An open protocol that makes access rights and their conditions machine-readable and verifiable. One pass, recognised by every participating service — online, and in cinemas, theatres and festivals.

Content and autonomy stay with each organisation. The protocol only adds the common layer.

How it works

Four elements, one verifiable flow.

  • Rights holder

    Creates the offer and sets its rules.

  • Content offer

    A work, an experience or a bundle.

  • Authorised user

    Holds the pass.

  • Conditions

    Duration, scope, transfer or sharing, organisation rules.

Open interfaces for issuance, verification and provider integration.

Principles

Built to be adopted, not owned.

  • Open standard, not a platform

    Anyone can build compatible tools, without a central operator's permission.

  • Ledger only where it helps

    Blockchain only where it adds integrity, traceability or verification — never as an end in itself.

  • Privacy by design

    No unnecessary publication of personal data.

  • No vendor lock-in

    Portability, open interfaces, independent deployment.

  • Rights holders in control

    Institutions and creators keep their content and their rules.

  • Interoperable by design

    Different implementations, the same common rules.

Who benefits

Value on every side of access.

  • Audiences

    Transfer, share or resell access when it's not in use.

  • Institutions & rights holders

    Automated royalties on secondary transactions and transparent revenue sharing.

  • European platforms

    Lower barriers to entry, more competition and innovation.

Open by default

Released as digital commons.

Every output is published under a recognised free or open source licence.

  • Protocol specification

    The technical standard.

  • Reference implementation

    Working code for the specification.

  • Interoperability & conformance

    Materials to check that implementations agree.

  • Documentation & guide

    Accessible to organisations with limited technical capacity.

Expected impact

A stronger, fairer European cultural ecosystem.

  • Ecosystem strengthening

    A connected network of European institutions and platforms.

  • Innovation leadership

    Shared standards for distribution and rights management.

  • Economic fairness

    Transparent compensation for creators and organisations.

  • Access and visibility

    Integrated experiences and more discoverable European content.

Culture is the first use case of a broader open model for verifiable access — wherever independent services need to cooperate without centralising control.
